Before you walk in

What am I up against?

Pick the response before the collision. Then plow.

  • PowerDENY · DEFY · GRINArrogance, lateness, phone out. Small playful denial (“Not yet.”), light defiance (“I only have twelve.”). No deference, no small talk.
  • TimePRIZE“Only ten minutes.” Don’t thank them. “I don’t work like that. Can you keep a schedule?” They’ll offer thirty.
  • AnalystNUMBERS → STORYSomeone starts calculating. Three numbers, redirect to fit. If they keep digging, the intrigue story.
  • TensionPUSH · PULLFlat faces, polite nods, no questions: there are no stakes. Run one push-pull loop (“we may not be right for each other… but if we were”) or take something back. Then return to the pitch.
  • MovementNOT TWO PICTURESBrains see motion and go blind to static comparisons. Never show before and after side by side; show the market moving from the old way to yours, pushed by the three forces.
  • Twenty minutes5 · 10 · 2 · 3Say it first: “I’ve only got about twenty minutes to give you the big idea.” You and the idea (5, track record under 2, why now from three forces), budget and secret sauce (10), the deal (2), stacked frames (3). Then leave.
  • Beta trapsIGNORE POLITELYGuest book, badge, stale coffee, the low chair, the cell with four plastic chairs. Walk through them unaffected. Arrive on time; lateness gives away power before you start.
  • StatusSITUATIONALYou can’t beat global status (wealth, rank). You can seize situational status: small defiance, then move fast into your domain where you are unassailable (local star power), then prize, then make them qualify to you: “Remind me why I’d want to do business with you?”
  • Best ideaWHEN THEY ARE BIGGERWhen the competition has size, pedigree and a longer track record, change what the room is choosing: not the biggest team but the best idea. “This is not about who is most charming or most skilled in finance; it is about who has the right ideas.” Then describe the big firms as large, young and well-paid, and let them spend their hour digging out of it.
  • Only likeTHE CLOSE“The only thing worse than an idea you hate is an idea you just like. If you only like us, throw us out, and that would be the right thing.” Love or nothing; it removes the lukewarm yes and leaves the room with a real choice. Then withdraw: “when the time is right, come to our office.”
  • The stackINTRIGUE · PRIZE · TIME · MORALWhen the room goes analytical after the deal is offered, do not answer the drill. Run four frames in quick succession: the cliffhanger story, “so many buyers, only one of me” as a statement, the deal train (a real date you don’t control), and what the right thing is and who it protects. Then pull away. Wanting, not understanding, is what you are building.
  • AuthorityMORAL BEATS ITThe surgeon’s frame: do what I say, defer to my expertise, accept my conclusions. Wealth and credentials cannot break it; Mother Teresa broke it in twenty days with four rules that outranked theirs. Do not react to their status. Say the right thing and who it protects, then ask them to sign the sheet.
  • Hot vs coldCHOCOLATE · SPINACHThe neocortex can only like your idea; the croc brain can want it. Features and benefits are spinach. Stack frames so the room forms a preference before it evaluates, then hand the cold deciders their numbers card for the matrix they will run after you leave.
  • Thirty secondsDESIRE → ACTIONAfter the stack you hold attention for about half a minute. Spend it on the ask, as a statement with a date and a name, not on a recap.
  • Absent Mr. Big15 MIN“I can wait fifteen minutes, then I have to leave.” If he doesn’t show, leave politely and reschedule on your turf.

When the room goes polite

Attention is desire plus tension.

Novelty gives them something to want. Tension gives them something to lose. All pull and no push reads as neediness; all push and they leave. Use one loop when the faces go flat.

  • LowPUSH · PAUSE · PULL“There’s a real possibility we might not be right for each other.” Pause. Let it land. “But if this did work, our two sides together would be something.”
  • MediumVALUES“Ideas are common. What counts is who runs them, and if we don’t share that view it will never work between us.” Pause. “But that’s crazy to think. You clearly value people over slides. We have that in common.”
  • HighPACK UP“From what I’m getting back, this isn’t a fit. You should only do deals you believe in. Let’s wrap for now and get together on the next one.” Stand, start packing, and mean it.
  • Take it backTHE CONFERENCE MOVETwenty-five analysts, no questions. “Since you can’t think of anything to ask, let me have those books back.” Collect them. Now they have something to lose; questions start.

Ready.

S — Set the frame

Time constraint pattern, said first, before the laptop opens: “Let’s get started. I’ve only got about twenty minutes to give you the big idea, which leaves us time to talk it over before I have to get out of here.” It puts them at ease (they are not trapped for an hour), it signals a pro, and it sets the prize and time frames in one breath.

Then the four phases: you and the big idea (5 min, with your track record under 2), budget and secret sauce (10), the deal (2), stack frames for hot cognition (3). Attention runs dry at twenty and the brain starts forgetting what it already learned.

If they say “we only have ten minutes”: no thanks, no gratitude. Smile: “I don’t work like that. I only need eight, but I need the right people in the room.”

If the decision maker is missing: “I can wait fifteen minutes. After that, let’s reschedule and I’ll host.” Mean it.

Mission

Names a status the croc brain understands (best-informed) and a rival it understands (the utility). No acronyms. A flag officer can repeat it.

Alternates to test:

  • “No Navy utility bill goes unread.”
  • “Turn every utility bill into a decision.”
  • “The Navy pays for every kilowatt-hour. ANCCRai makes sure it pays the right price.”

Pause two seconds after the headline before the subline.

Why now · the three market forces

Nobody funds an old deal. Before the idea, the room needs to know it came from forces you saw first: economic, social, technology. Then the backstory in three beats: what changed, what it did to cost and demand, and the window it opened.

Talk track: “Three things changed in the last year, and together they opened a window.” Say the three, then one sentence on the window: “Utilities are filing rate cases faster than anyone at an installation can read them, and for the first time the tools to read them exist.”

Keep your own background to one great thing, under two minutes, before this slide. People average what they hear about you; a second, weaker item lowers the average.

T — Tell the story

Three concrete chunks, nothing abstract, nothing you can’t defend. Each line is something someone in the room already knows is true.

Talk track: “Nothing here is a criticism. The data is good. There is simply no one whose full-time job is reading it at the account level.” Then stop.

R — Reveal the intrigue

The idea in one minute (idea introduction pattern): “For installation energy managers who are dissatisfied with reading utility bills by hand, ANCCRai is an AI analyst that reads every bill, matches every tariff and flags the ones that are wrong. Unlike dashboards, every number shows the field it came from.” Who it’s for, what it is, who you compete with, what it does better. No details yet; the temperature is still cool.

The novelty is the yellow tag, not the four modules. Provenance answers the 10-29 room’s biggest worry before they raise it.

Talk track: point at the tag. “Ask it where a number came from and it shows you the Maximo field.” The only place you may open the app, for one question only.

If someone drills in here: open Numbers. If they keep going: open Intrigue.

R — Proof plan

Three years of Cheetah Mechanics data including interval data, agreed 10-29. Known answers make the first win undeniable.

Analyst defence: “The full data dictionary ships at day 30. Today I want to show you where the bodies are.” Keep the room in the story.

O — Offer the prize

Reverse qualification. They are applying to be first. Say the three criteria, then: “Which site meets all three?”

Statement, not question: “A lot of installations, one of me.” Said with a grin. No trial closes anywhere in this deck.

N — Hook point · G — Get the deal

Hook point: the first validated number is 30 days away and the only thing between them and it is three yeses they control.

The task: prizing says make them do something to earn it. Ask for the champion’s name out loud. Write it down in front of them. Then stop talking.

If they want time: “What specifically do you need to see?” Set the next date before leaving the room.

Analyst frame · summary card

Hot and cold cognition can’t coexist. The moment the room starts calculating, the pitch cools. Answer fast, at the highest level, then redirect to the relationship.

Replace these three when real figures exist (annual spend in scope, flagged dollars, validated savings). Until then these are the three facts you can say in one breath without being wrong.

Analyst frame · intrigue story

Deploy only when the analyst frame appears and the summary card didn’t hold. Open with “This reminds me of…” and stop before the reveal. Go straight back to the pitch. Finish the story after the ask.

Write yours here. Candidates from your world: a night an installation lost power, a bill that was wrong by a lot and nobody caught it, a Marine Corps moment where the clock mattered. It must be true and you must be in it.

Time frame · you end it

Time frames arrive after you have control, when attention cools. If you wait for them to call it, you’re reacting and the frame is gone. Watch for phones, side conversations, the “so, in summary” tone.

Running long or talking faster signals need. End on your terms and the interested ones ask for the follow-up.

The attention cocktail

Dopamine is anticipation of reward; novelty releases it (a demo, a metaphor, an unexpected gain). Expected rewards give none; a promised reward that doesn’t arrive drains it and the room starts forgetting what you said. Norepinephrine is alertness; it comes from stakes, from something that can be lost. Together they hold for about twenty minutes, then fall out of balance.

A pitch narrative is a series of tension loops: push, then pull; create, then resolve. If it was all smiles and nods and ended with “that was really nice,” there was no tension and no deal. Don’t manipulate; keep them alert. And remember Seinfeld: even he gets three free minutes, then the material has to earn the rest.

The twenty-minute pitch

Watson and Crick pitched the structure of DNA in five minutes read aloud. Nothing you have needs an hour. Attention expires at about twenty minutes and then runs backwards.

Phase 1 (5 min): your track record in under two minutes, one great thing only, then the why-now frame built from three forces (economic, social, technology) and the idea’s backstory. Phase 2 (10): budget and secret sauce. Phase 3 (2): offer the deal. Phase 4 (3): stack frames for hot cognition. Then stop, and pull away.

Run this on the phone face-up during the pitch. It will tell you when to move and when to leave.

Odds · how the number is built

No black box. Every point comes from a rule in the method: frame match, status setup, decision maker present, time fit, prep done, room weight (rank-weighted sliders), and the croc read of the narrative text (short sentences, concrete numbers, no jargon, statements not questions, one time-bound ask).

Scenarios overlay the plan without changing it, so you can see what one absence or one prepared story is worth.

Uncertainty. Until outcomes are recorded the number carries a wide band. Record won or lost on each pitch (in the plan) and the band narrows and the calibration line shows how far the engine has been running from reality. Treat the score as a ranking tool, not a promise.

Frame cheat sheet

Plowing: at first contact you’ll feel it in your stomach. That is the signal to commit, not retreat. Keep moving forward. No self-doubt out loud.

Rules once you own it: everything in the room is about the ask; something happens for you every few minutes; the meeting ends when you say.

Dominating the frame is the means, not the win. Keep it fun.

Seizing situational status, in order: ignore power rituals and beta traps; be unaffected by their global status; perpetrate small denials and defiances; move the discussion into your domain; apply the prize frame; get them to say something that qualifies your higher status. Status is temporary. It resets every time you leave the room.

The apple rule: a well-timed, friendly, shocking act dethrones the king in one stroke. Then ignore every thread that doesn’t advance the deal and magnify the ones that do. Pull away the moment the pitch ends.
